## Why I Chose Software Development
Why did I choose software development? When I was choosing which career path I wanted to go and study, I knew computing would be it. I enjoyed and was good at computer-related subjects. I had dabbled in coding at high school, and the feeling of when you finally get something to work is the best, so I chose to study to become a software developer.

So, what do I like about coding? I promote myself as someone who enjoys creating solutions for everyday problems. Which is true, why develop something for naught? I don’t necessarily enjoy writing thousands of lines of code. I enjoy crafting code into an application that will solve a complex idea. I take pride in crafting code that is maintainable. 

So how did I become a technical lead? I am someone who likes to take ownership. I enjoy knowing the whole system and how it fits together. I also enjoy coding. Basically, I started doing the technical lead work and then was recognised with the title. 

## Becoming a Technical Lead

Once I did have the title, my role did change in ways I did not expect in my day-to-day job. 

### I Do Less Doing
I now do a lot less of the actual doing. I still fill in gaps where there is a skill shortage or lower urgency but high importance work that doesn’t block other developers. But overall, a lot of my role has turned into enabling my team to do what they do best. Also guiding them on what work needs to be done and being the in-between of the developers and the stakeholders. 

### So Many Meetings
So many more meetings. Ad hoc meetings with my team to explain part of the application/architecture that they are still getting their heads around or helping them past issues they are trying to solve. And stakeholder meetings to get an understanding of their expectations, help prioritise work and overall updates on the application we are building for them. 

### Learning to Delegate
At the beginning I did struggle with this.  I have had to learn when and what to delegate. I have had to think about the whole future of the application. If I am the only one who can do this part, how healthy is this? Who should I train up so they can start to help in that area? 

### I also do
* More architectural and planning
* Mentoring my team
* A lot of code reviews

## What I Expected vs What I Actually Do

Looking back before I was a Technical Lead, what did I expect versus what I am doing? I honestly thought the role would be more technical guidance with more ownership of the application (in my case) or parts of an application for bigger systems. What did surprise me was how much I have had to step away from code. I have become an enabler rather than a doer. Also spending a lot of time trying to understand stakeholder expectations and protecting the other developers from indecisions. Now I am doing it, there are parts I really enjoy. I feel a sense of accomplishment when my team members achieve things. I am getting better and better at negotiating time frames and priorities with stakeholders. 

## I Still Want to Write Code

If tomorrow morning I went into work and was no longer expected to code, I would miss the creative side of the doing. Architecting a system, planning out how to do something are not as rewarding as actually doing the work. Seeing my team achieve is motivating, but I would miss having my own happy dance when working on a very hard challenge and finally getting it to work. 

## What Hands-On Technical Leadership Means to Me

I would prefer to be a hands-on technical lead. Someone who:
* writes code
* enables team members
* plans and designs systems
* mentors and upskills other team members
* reviews code
* makes technical decisions
* manages stakeholders

I understand that most of my time would not be in the code, but I believe it is important to be in the code to keep my skills up and help my team members flourish. 

## Finding the Balance

The hardest part is to balance how much time I get to code. As I have mentioned, I have overcome this by choosing tasks that are high importance but low urgency, that will not block others from achieving their work. I also don’t want to be the hoarder of knowledge. So, I may start something where my team has a technical skill gap, but I try to train them up as soon as I have done the base work so they can continue tasks related to that area. 

## I’m Still Learning

Of course I am still learning. I am still figuring out how to best communicate with stakeholders without going into too much detail. I am still figuring out when to delegate to my team members but also not overwhelm them with all the knowledge in my head. I do try to document everything I do (future self always appreciates this), but as others haven’t always been part of the thinking journey, this may not be enough. 

## So, I Still Like Writing Code

Overall, I do enjoy being a technical lead. I love seeing people around me grow and become more and more confident. I enjoy planning a whole system, not just a tiny part I am working on. And currently I do still get to code, although a lot less than I would want.
